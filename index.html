    How Pip works

    Reading apps usually ask you to photograph a page or swap the book for a screen. Pip keeps the real book in your hands and helps one word at a time, the way a parent sitting next to you would.

    1. Hand tracking: an on-device AI model (MediaPipe Hand Landmarker) finds your index fingertip in every camera frame.
    2. Pointing: when your fingertip rests in one place for about half a second, Pip looks at the line of text just above it.
    3. Reading: an on-device text-recognition model (Tesseract) reads that small area, and Pip picks the word sitting right above your finger.
    4. Helping: it highlights the word, says it, breaks it into syllables to sound out, and saves it to My words for practice.

    Private by design

    Both AI models are downloaded once with the app and run inside your browser. Camera frames stay in memory for a moment and are never stored or sent anywhere. The page’s security policy only lets it load its own files, so there is nowhere to send them. My words lives only on this device.

    Good to know

    • Works best with printed text, good light, and the camera held steady above the page.
    • Handwriting and fancy fonts are harder to read.
    • Syllables follow classroom rules and won’t be right for every word.
    • The voice comes from your device, so it differs between phones and computers.
